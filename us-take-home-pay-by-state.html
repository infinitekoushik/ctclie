Who keeps the most, and who keeps the least

At $100,000, the eight states with no income tax keep $79,180 a year for a single filer. Among the states that do tax wages, Arizona keeps the most at $77,074, and California keeps the least at $72,811. That is $6,369 a year between the top and the bottom, or about $531 a month.

The order is not the same at every pay level. At $100,000 and $150,000 California keeps the least of the 26 states. At $75,000 it is Minnesota.

Payroll programs count too

Income tax is not the only state deduction. At $100,000 these state payroll programs come out of a single filer’s pay: Washington $1,387, California $1,300, New Jersey $610, Massachusetts $460, New York $443, Colorado $440, Minnesota $440.

Washington is the clearest example. It has no income tax, yet its Paid Leave and WA Cares deductions cost about $1,387 a year, so it keeps $1,387 less than the eight states with no payroll programs.

What these numbers leave out

Local income taxes, 401(k) contributions, health insurance, and credits are not included. California and New York are estimates. We support 26 states so far and add each remaining state only after checking it against that state’s own 2026 rules, because many states changed rates or deductions during 2026. Sources and dates for every rule are on our methodology page. These are estimates, not tax advice.

Questions people ask

Eight states charge no income tax on wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas and Wyoming. A single filer takes home about $79,180 a year ($6,598 a month) in each. Washington is close behind at $77,793, because of its payroll programs.
On our numbers, California state income tax is about $5,069 and its disability insurance is $1,300, so a single filer takes home about $72,811, which is $6,369 a year less than in a state with no income tax.
