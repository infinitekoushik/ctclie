The honest pros and cons

Staying in India
Family nearby. No long flights home, and parents close if they need you.
Cheaper services. Cooks, cleaners and drivers cost a fraction of what they do in the US.
Cheaper housing. In many cities a flat costs far less than a US house.
No visa worries. No H-1B lottery and no green card backlog.
A growing market. Strong demand for tech and finance talent in the big cities.
Simple tax at mid salaries. On the new regime, salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax.
Moving to the US
Higher pay at the top. Senior tech salaries and stock are well above Indian levels.
Career credibility. US experience is recognised worldwide.
Stock and retirement matching. RSUs and 401(k) matches are common.
Dollar savings. $1,000 sent home is about ₹95,500 at our assumed rate.
A large job market. More employers for specialised roles.
Things to weigh. Visa risk, health insurance costs and distance from family.

Questions people ask

About ₹1,44,035 a month in hand from 20 LPA buys roughly what $5,279 a month does in the US if living costs are 3.5 times lower in India. That needs a US gross salary of about $77,000 in Texas or $84,000 in California, before 401(k) and health premiums. The gap between cities matters a lot, so try the calculator with your own numbers.
It depends on your field and stage. Senior tech pay in the US is higher than anything you can get in India today, so the money can be very good. In other fields the gap after living costs is much smaller. Also count the H-1B lottery, the green card backlog for Indians, and being far from family.
Prices in India are lower, but by how much depends on where you live and how you live. We let you pick 2.5x (big cities in both countries), 3.5x (a typical middle) or 4.5x (a modest life in India). It is a rough assumption, not a measured number, so check how the result changes across all three.
As an NRI you can invest through NRE or NRO accounts. Money in an NRE account can be sent back freely, while NRO money has limits and tax rules. Some Indian fund houses do not accept investors who live in the US or Canada, and US tax rules can make Indian mutual funds costly for US taxpayers, so check before you invest and talk to a CA or CPA.
Compare the rupees that arrive, not just the fee. Banks often build a margin into the exchange rate. Services such as Wise show the live rate and the fee upfront, so you can see the total. Check the amount received on a few options before you send.
In this example, 30 LPA gives about ₹2,02,977 a month in hand. $100K in California gives about $6,068 a month, which is worth about ₹1,65,559 of spending in India at a 3.5x gap. So India comes out ahead on day-to-day spending, while the US wins on dollar savings and career upside.
